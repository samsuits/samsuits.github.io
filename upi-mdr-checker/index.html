Customers never pay MDR directly, even on chargeable transactions. It's settled between the merchant's bank and the payment ecosystem. If a merchant asks you to pay extra or split a payment because of this, that goes against RBI/NPCI's rules. Banks have been told merchants must not pass MDR on to customers.
Small P2PM merchants stay at zero MDR on every transaction, of any size, as long as total UPI QR receipts stay at or under ₹1,00,000 for that calendar month. Crossing the limit mid-month means transactions after that point (above ₹2,000 each) start attracting the standard MDR. It isn't retroactive on what you already received.

What is MDR?

Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) is the transaction fee or service charge that a business or merchant pays to their acquiring bank or payment service provider (PSP) for processing digital payments, such as UPI, debit cards, or credit cards.

How does it work under the new UPI circular?

While standard customer-to-customer transfers and small transactions remain free, the newer framework introduces structured, tiered fee updates for high-value commerce to sustain and maintain the digital payments infrastructure.

Important Rules & Frameworks:

  • Free for Consumers: Customers never pay any MDR directly. It is strictly settled on the business/merchant side. Passing it on to users is prohibited by the RBI.
  • The Small Merchant Cap: Businesses receiving up to ₹1,00,000 per month via UPI QR are classified as small merchants and are completely exempt from MDR.
  • Sub-₹2,000 Rule: Any payment of ₹2,000 or less to a merchant is automatically exempt from MDR, regardless of category.
  • Commercial Categories: Transactions exceeding ₹2,000 attract a tier-based MDR (e.g., standard categories are 0.4% capped at ₹300, while essentials are a flat ₹5 fee).
Not official NPCI/bank tooling. Rules can be revised before or after the Oct 15 rollout. Double-check with your PSP for your exact merchant category.
